Evidence Reliability in Explainable Data Drift Monitoring
Saneedullah Ahmed Bilal
This Phase 2 submission develops a technically justified methodology for evaluating evidence reliability
in explainable data drift monitoring. It contains four deliverables: an extended review of eight ad-
ditional 2025–2026 research publications; a refined research gap; a twelve-step proposed methodology;
and methodology diagrams. Published findings are distinguished from proposed project experiments
throughout. No model implementation, completed experiment or achieved project result is claimed.
Continuity with Phase 1 and Scope Refinement
Phase 1 identified the need to connect drift detection, feature-level explanation and predictive conse-
quences, while recognising limitations in reference maintenance and delayed-label evidence. Its broad
direction included image representations, adaptive learning and analyst evaluation. Phase 2 will refine
that direction into a controlled tabular-classification study of whether available monitoring evidence
supports a cohort-specific statement of material excess predictive error. This narrower scope preserves
the central interpretation problem while making independent truth, matched comparisons and repro-
ducibility feasible. Image-domain transfer, adaptive intervention and human decision benefit will require
separate subsequent studies; the present protocol will not claim to have evaluated them [1–5].
The submission first examines the additional literature, then develops the gap synthesis and research
question, the proposed experiment and its diagrammatic specification. This sequence distinguishes the
evidence motivating the study from the methods that the project proposes to evaluate [6–8].

4\nCross-Corpus Methodological Synthesis
Evaluation design and reference meaning.S1 evaluates whether detectors recover controlled
changes, whereas S2 shows that the samples defining a window can change what the detection prob-
lem means. Together, they motivate a distinction between a detector’s native input-comparison target
and the classifier’s predictive-risk target. S4 adds a computational perspective: batching can alter the
power–runtime trade-off, but its simulation results depend on sampling assumptions. The proposed
study will therefore retain reference-specific input truth, one fixed predictive-risk reference and explicit
calibration costs [6,7,9].
Label access and recurrence.S3 supplies a sensitive prediction-uncertainty signal, but its true-class
score requires observed labels. S7 also uses labels to evaluate nonconformity and the suitability of
previously trained models. Neither result supports treating a confidence curve as evidence of correctness
before labels arrive. The present study will record prediction and label-release times separately, and will
isolate delayed evidence availability while keeping classifiers frozen. Model reuse and adaptation will
remain distinct research questions [8,12].
Explanation and intervention.S5 studies causal drift explanations under a structural model, while
S6 uses explanation shifts to trigger forecasting updates. Their outputs have different evidential status:
a graph-based explanation depends on causal assumptions, and a reduction in forecasting error combines
detection with the subsequent learning policy. The proposed marginal feature display will consequently
be evaluated as distributional screening, not as a causal explanation or proof that retraining would
help [10,11].
Feedback and generalization.S8 requires deliberate prediction interventions to distinguish perfor-
mative feedback from intrinsic change. Its failure under severe imbalance illustrates how a successful
controlled mechanism may still transfer poorly. Across S1–S8, recurring limitations concern the avail-
ability of evaluation truth, the sampling and reference assumptions, the distinction between detection
and action, and the target population represented by the experiment. These patterns justify controlled
independent experiments accompanied by separately qualified dependent and natural-data analyses; they
do not justify a universal superiority ranking [6–13].

8\nResearch Gap Refinement and Justification
What has already been done.Published research has established controlled drift benchmarks, demon-
strated window-dependent interpretation, developed uncertainty-based monitoring and investigated the
relationship between explanation, recurrence and model adaptation. Phase 1 reviewed representation-
based detection, selective feature monitoring and proactive adaptation as complementary foundations.
These are existing contributions on which the proposed evaluation will build, rather than capabilities
already implemented by this project [1–3,6–12].
What approaches currently exist.The literature includes error- or uncertainty-based signals that
require labels, input-distance methods that can operate without labels, adaptive reference windows,
recurrent-model selection, SHAP-triggered retraining and causal intervention analysis. Their evidence
requirements and output targets differ. The proposed study will compare simple distributional signals
with released-label loss evidence under matched data access; it will not treat the literature’s heteroge-
neous scores as interchangeable measures of predictive harm [7–12].
What the limitations are.A detected input difference does not imply a harmful loss change; an
adaptive learner’s accuracy also reflects its update policy. Window boundaries affect the population
comparison, and true-class scores cannot be computed before labels arrive. Explanation fidelity, drift
sensitivity and predictive-harm correctness consequently require distinct evidence. Natural streams
frequently lack independently certified event truth [5–8,10,12].
What remains insufficiently investigated.Within the reviewed protocols, no focal experiment
jointly isolates fixed/rolling input references, one invariant excess-risk target, independently bounded
harm truth and matched interval-based gated versus nongated cohort decisions at delayed checkpoints.
S4–S8 address neighbouring questions, but their batching, causal, retraining, model-reuse or feedback
objectives do not supply this particular contrast. This is a bounded empirical question, not a claim of
universal algorithmic novelty [6–13].
Why the gap matters.Acting on an input alarm as though it certifies model harm can produce
unnecessary investigation or false reassurance. Conversely, presenting old released labels as evidence
about a newer cohort confuses evidence age with current validity. Measuring correct and wrong addi-
tional decisions alongside deferral will expose these interpretation failures without assuming that every
additional verdict is useful or that every alarm warrants retraining [7,8,11,14].
How the proposed research will address it.The methodology will freeze classifiers, separate in-
put and label channels, maintain a fixed predictive-risk reference, and compare precisely defined verdict
policies on the same cohorts. Synthetic mechanisms will supply bounded truth; natural cases will sup-
port qualified observation. Reference-specific localization, disjoint calibration, seed-level uncertainty,
ablations and failure analysis will make the contribution testable. The question will permit null find-
ings, including no reliable additional harm decisions or insufficient precision to support a practically
informative difference [5–8].
Refined Research Question and Study Objectives
For frozen tabular classifiers, how do fixed versus rolling input references affect the relia-
bility of distributional alarms as proxies for excess predictive risk, and which correct and
incorrect cohort-specific harm declarations are recovered when delayed loss evidence can
support them without an input alarm?
Distributional detection, prediction uncertainty and model adaptation answer related but distinct ques-
tions. Reference-window choice already affects what change is observable, and a more sensitive signal
does not by itself establish material excess risk. The proposed contribution will be a controlled com-
parison of alarm interpretation and matched verdict policies, with independently bounded risk truth,
explicit deferral and fixed assessment times. It will be an evaluation contribution, rather than a claim
to invent a new drift detector or to establish intervention benefit [6–8].
Study objectives.The first objective will be to quantify how reference maintenance changes the
relationship between an input alarm and fixed-reference excess predictive risk. The second will be to
9\nestimatethecorrectandincorrectadditionalharmdeclarationsobtainedwhenaninputalarmisnolonger
required for an otherwise supported harm verdict. The third will be to specify a cohort-aligned display
of input evidence, label availability and predictive-risk uncertainty, and to characterize its statistical and
observational failure modes. These objectives will concern evidence validity rather than demonstrated
analyst benefit [4,7,8].

10\nDeliverable 3 — Proposed Research Methodology
The proposed pipeline will progress from auditable observations and frozen representations to parallel
input- and loss-evidence channels, matched experimental comparisons, and uncertainty-qualified research
outputs. The independent synthetic experiment will be the primary evidence source because it supplies
known generating mechanisms and bounded risk truth. Dependent controls will test robustness; natural
case studies will examine descriptive transfer without inventing population-harm labels. The twelve steps
below specify proposed work and the assumptions under which its conclusions would be interpretable
[5–7].

Step 2 — Data Collection
Controlled synthetic streams will provide event and variable truth. Electricity and the NOAA Weather
benchmark described in the stream-benchmarking literature will be the planned natural case studies.
11\nEach will require an auditable chronological order, a documented label definition and an identifiable data
version before inclusion. Covertype will supply a separate constructed multiclass replay; its source order
will not be interpreted as deployment time. A data card will record acquisition conditions, checksums,
class counts, missingness and partition boundaries. These retrospective datasets will support cross-
domain case comparison, not a claim of representative deployment sampling [5].
The proposed data contract will assign an immutable prediction identifier, cohort identifier, model and
preprocessing versions, input time, prediction and label-release time to each record. Labels will join one-
to-one by prediction identifier, never by row position or nearest timestamp. Exact duplicate identifiers
will be rejected; equal feature values alone will not identify duplicates. Conflicting labels, unavailable
timestamps and invalid schemas will have explicit failure codes. Naturally observed labels will not be
described as error-free population truth. Synthetic labels will follow the declared generator; natural label
definitions and known annotation limitations will be recorded before analysis [5,17].
Each final synthetic seed will reserve 10,000 observations for model fitting and a disjoint 10,000-
observation pre-change risk reference, shared across its six mechanisms and two reference policies. Its
two classifiers will fit the same model-training observations. Each mechanism will generate 60,000 test
observations; policies compared within that mechanism will receive exactly the same realized inputs
and labels. Mechanisms may share exogenous Gaussian innovations and uniform label-noise draws, but
their interventions will change the resulting observations. An additional independent 10,000-observation
development sample will fit the common synthetic representation and kernel bandwidth once. Separate
random-number namespaces will serve model development, score normalization, threshold selection,
held-out null validation, final streams and oracle evaluation. Five model-development seeds and 30 final
evaluation seeds will be prespecified. The precision planning in Step 9 will qualify conclusions at that
fixed budget; no final-test-dependent replication will occur [5,6].

12\nStep 3 — Data Preprocessing
For the synthetic study, imputation, numeric scaling and the kernel representation will be fitted on the
independent representation-development sample and frozen across all final seeds. Numeric missing values
will use fitted medians; scaled coordinates will be(xj−medianj)/IQRj, with a unit denominator when
the fitted interquartile range is zero. Categories will use a fixed one-hot vocabulary with explicit missing
and other-category levels. Model configurations will be selected on the five disjoint development seeds:
each classifier will fit the first 80% of its training partition and validate on the final 20%. The selected
configuration will then fit each final seed’s complete model-training partition. Shared preprocessing
will therefore be independent of final model selection, risk-reference labels and test outcomes. For each
natural dataset, candidate preprocessing will fit only the fitting portion of its chronological validation
split; after selection, it will be refitted on the full training prefix. Predefined missingness flags will be
retained, and encoded contributions will be mapped back to original variables [5].
An entirely missing numeric fitting column will receive a fixed zero imputation and unit scale, with
its missingness indicator retained; no test statistic will supply its fitting value. Nonfinite numeric val-
ues and schema/type violations will be recorded as data-contract failures rather than silently coerced
into ordinary measurements. No outlier clipping, target-derived feature, future-based interpolation or
outcome-dependent row deletion will be used. Repeated records, temporal aggregation and source iden-
tifiers will be audited before partitioning to prevent an artificial chronological split from concealing
duplicated observations or future information [5,16].
The primary cohort will containb=b 0 = 1,000consecutive observations. Cohort sizes 500 and 2,000
will be sensitivities; every analysis will use nonoverlapping cohorts with strideb. Both input-reference
policies will use a fixed size of 1,000 observations: the final 1,000 rows of the held-out reference prefix,
or the immediately preceding 1,000 stream observations. The first rolling reference will therefore also
come from that prefix. All observations used for a rolling reference will precede the current cohort.
The held-out 10,000-observation predictive-risk reference will remain fixed for both policies. Incomplete
cohorts, missing labels and exclusion reasons will be recorded [1,5,18].
Cohort membership will be fixed asWs ={(s−1)b+ 1,...,sb}, with membership defined before looking
at losses or alarms. A trailing partial cohort will be excluded identically for every policy and listed with
its observation count; the core 60,000-observation streams are divisible by every planned cohort size. The
preprocessing specification will retain the fitted values, category vocabulary, original-to-encoded feature
map and reference-row identifiers, making both transformation leakage and accidental reference overlap
auditable [5].

The primary design will cross two input references with delaysd∈{0,2,5}and two frozen classifiers

Step 10 — Ablation and Component Analysis
The input-monitor ablation will remove joint MMD and recalibrate the remaining maximum under the
same stationary alert target. It may affect input alarms, screened-feature sets and gated positive verdicts,
while leaving the evidence-separated loss verdicts identical. The primary matched interval comparison
will isolate gating of harm declarations. The strict all-verdict gate will be a separate ablation. Point-loss
andpoint-gatedcomparisonswillexaminetheeffectsofremovinguncertaintyhandlingandofinterpreting
absent alarms as below-margin; they will not be presented as additional pure gating contrasts [1,19].
An uncertainty sensitivity will vary the prespecified interval levels. Window-size, harm-margin and
reference sensitivities will preserve observation-level data and seeds. Window-size runs will keep delays,
onset times and assessment deadlines fixed in observation units, rather than inadvertently changing the
latency treatment by redefining a window. No ablation will infer unavailable labels or introduce adaptive
retraining [6,14].
Component experiments will change one defined element while holding the stream, classifier, risk refer-
ence and assessment schedule fixed. Removing MMD will change input evidence but leave the separated
loss verdict identical; changing only the harm gate will leave the interval identical; replacing the interval
by a point rule will intentionally change uncertainty handling. Sensitivities will vary one factor at a
time around the primary configuration unless an interaction is explicitly part of the core crossed design.
23\nConfidence, margin, window, amplitude and event-tolerance variants will not automatically form an un-
affordable full Cartesian product. A component record will specify the changed element, recalibration
requirement, invariant quantities and intended interpretation before that analysis begins [6,14,19].
Step 11 — Error / Failure Analysis
The proposed failure catalogue will distinguish harmless input alarms, missed marginal changes, missed
dependence changes, false reassurance, unsupported harm declarations, interval overcoverage or under-
coverage, prolonged deferral and reference assimilation of changed inputs. Each case will retain the
cohort, input-reference membership, label-arrival times, expected-risk truth interval and realized losses.
Diagnostics will distinguish deficient evidence from an incorrect decisive statement [1,2,5,14].
Conditional-only cases will test the limit of input monitoring rather than be used to claim universal
input detectability. Dependence-only controls will be checked analytically and by univariate distribution
diagnostics. Natural deterioration will not automatically be assigned a certified drift cause. Selective or
permanently missing labels, unseen classes, analyst usefulness and optimal retraining action will remain
outside the core study’s claims [4,5,14].
Failure analysis will first enumerate every qualifying case and its denominator, then provide the first
chronological example of each prespecified failure category within each mechanism, classifier and ref-
erence policy. Additional extreme cases may illustrate severity but will be labelled selected examples
rather than representative population evidence. Each trace will show current and reference membership,
released-label fraction, interval position relative to the margin, oracle bounds when available and the
identified failing rule or assumption, with an explicit unknown diagnosis when the available evidence
cannot establish one. This will separate a detector’s native-task failure, a wrong harm interpretation,
legitimate deferral and a data-pipeline fault [5,7,16].
Step 12 — Final Research Output
At Phase 2, the submitted outputs will be the extended literature review, refined gap, proposed protocol
and methodology diagrams. The experiment configurations, comparative result tables and evidence
display described here will be specifications for subsequent research, not completed software or measured
project outcomes.
The planned outputs will be a reproducible protocol, data and configuration manifests, comparative
tables with uncertainty, a catalogue of interpretation failures and a proposed aligned visualization. The
display specification will connect each cohort’s input alarm and marginal feature evidence to released-
label coverage, interval-based harm status and evidence age. An internal consistency criterion will require
the displayed harm status to match the loss-only identity control [4,5].
The final conclusions will describe measured differences in incorrect claims, coverage and latency under
the tested conditions, including null or negative findings. Independent synthetic conclusions, dependent-
control diagnostics and natural case-study observations will remain separately qualified. No planned
display will be taken as evidence of improved analyst decisions, and no frozen-classifier comparison will
establish model-adaptation benefit [3–5].
The independent synthetic comparison will be the core experiment. Before final evaluation, development
timing will record the cost of null calibration and oracle sampling in bounded-memory batches; a resource
manifest will state hardware, library versions and elapsed-time limits. Reused input scores and regime-
risk estimates will avoid repeating identical calculations for label delays. Secondary sensitivities and
case studies will be scheduled after the core comparison, with any resource-limited omission declared
before inspecting that secondary outcome. A pre-evaluation feasibility review will estimate total core
work from measured development cost and will record a fixed resource allocation before final seeds are
opened. If the full core is unaffordable, a revised protocol will be documented before evaluation, rather
than dropping unfavourable conditions afterward. Infrastructure or convergence failure will be reported
separately from a scientific null. Every core comparison will use the prespecified final-evaluation data
and frozen configuration [5,6,9].
The intended industrial relevance will be traceable predictions and labels, versioned baselines, explicit
24\nevidence availability and documented limits of validity. These choices are consistent with the documen-
tation and validity principles in NIST AI RMF 1.0, particularly MEASURE 2.1 and 2.5, and with the
separation of prediction capture and ground-truth evaluation in operational monitoring documentation.
They will not constitute certification, production validation or a recommendation to deploy a particular
service. All numerical tolerances in this protocol will be research design choices, not prescribed industry
thresholds [16,24].
The proposed research package will link the protocol version, data provenance, split membership, seeds,
fitted-transform specification, model/calibration settings, analysis definitions and figure-generation
source. Its reporting templates will reserve separate fields for measured results, analytical guarantees,
exploratory observations and limitations. A reproducibility review will require every displayed cohort
verdict and aggregate denominator to be reconstructible from the same versioned evidence records. At
this phase, these items will remain a research specification; subsequent empirical work will be needed
to populate results and demonstrate reproducibility in execution [5,16].
